## Factor f4 (positive): Everyday interpersonal negotiation and emotional vulnerability

**Recommended label:** **Everyday interpersonal negotiation and emotional vulnerability**

This pole brings together discourse about managing relationships and social contact: recognising mistakes, seeking reassurance, maintaining or resisting intimacy, and attempting to support or recover another person. These concerns are often situated in ordinary routines—waking, making calls, talking, and moving through daily life. Emotional vulnerability is prominent, but the examples also include confident boundary-setting, partying, and the pressures of social attention. The pole is therefore broader than romantic sadness alone.

### Possible labels

| Label | Justification |
|---|---|
| **Everyday interpersonal negotiation and emotional vulnerability** | Best accommodates both the evaluative and affective loadings and the examples’ varied ways of managing relationships, contact, and emotional dependence. |
| **Relational uncertainty, regret, and repair** | Captures the clearest emotional pattern in the examples: uncertainty about affection, acknowledgement of mistakes, and efforts to regain connection or help someone. Less inclusive of the partying and celebrity-attention examples. |
| **Everyday contact and relationship management** | Emphasises calls, conversation, routines, and the regulation of interpersonal access. Broadly applicable, though it gives less prominence to the strongly loaded evaluative and affective vocabulary. |

### 1. Lexical evidence: evaluation, everyday situations, and connection

The strongest loading, **stupid (.89)**, makes negative evaluation particularly important. In the illustrated context, it supports self-reproach: “I was stupid, I was foolish” in *f4_pos_005*. However, the word can also evaluate other people or situations, so the full-subcorpus loading should not be restricted to romantic regret on the basis of this example alone. **Sad (.57)** reinforces an affective interpretation, although its contextual uses are not illustrated in the supplied excerpts.

A second cluster locates discourse in everyday circumstances:

- **Wake (.67)** marks waking routines but can also represent recognition or emotional awakening.
- **Phone (.47)** and **talk (.31)** concern interpersonal contact and conversational exchange.
- **Second (.75)** and **few (.68)** provide temporal or quantitative framing. These are highly loaded but semantically general: they support situated, everyday narration rather than independently identifying a particular emotional discourse.

Other loadings broaden the pole. **Road (.51)** can support literal movement or figurative trajectories; **save (.34)** introduces help, rescue, or dependence. **Cloud (.67)** is compatible with dreamlike or elevated imagery, illustrated by “clouds above” in *f4_pos_003*. **Gentleman (.53)** and **cool (.39)** potentially contribute social characterisation, conduct, or evaluation, but their uses cannot be securely specified from these excerpts.

Taken together, the loadings suggest **emotionally evaluated interpersonal experience embedded in ordinary situations**, rather than a narrowly defined breakup discourse.

### 2. Excerpt evidence: uncertainty, access, repair, and support

Several examples give this lexical configuration a clear relational interpretation:

- In *f4_pos_001*, **wake**, **few**, and **phone** frame the discovery of a partner’s absence, retrospective assessment of the relationship, and a request for renewed contact.
- In *f4_pos_005*, **stupid** expresses acknowledged wrongdoing, while **phone** represents the intimate companionship now lost: “Who’s gonna talk to me on the phone till the sun comes up?”
- In *f4_pos_003*, **wake** and **phone** accompany uncertainty about reciprocal love and difficulty initiating contact.
- In *f4_pos_006*, **phone** introduces a troubled existing relationship, while **wake** becomes a metaphor for recognising the speaker as the appropriate partner.
- In *f4_pos_008* and *009*, **road** and **save** connect uncertain trajectories with emotional reliance or attempted intervention. The latter extends the pole beyond romance into friendship, blame, and the difficulty of helping another person.

The remaining examples prevent an exclusively sorrowful interpretation. In *f4_pos_002*, **second**, **few**, and **phone** occur within social arrangements, sexual pursuit, and the assertion of boundaries. In *f4_pos_004*, **wake** and **phone** belong to partying and unwanted advances; in *007*, they accompany the demands of public attention. In *010*, they frame affectionate everyday contact and a temporarily unsuccessful call.

Thus, **contact is not simply desired: it is sought, missed, refused, interrupted, or experienced as demanding**.

### 3. Decade pattern

The authoritative decade means rank this pole as follows:

| Decade | Mean f4 |

| **2000s** | **0.309** |

The **2000s show the strongest mean association** and supply all ten illustrative excerpts. The pattern suggests a decline in the 2010s followed by a partial recovery in the 2020s—not a continuous downward trajectory.

The substantial within-decade standard deviations, approximately **0.58–0.71**, indicate considerable variation. Without inferential tests, these differences should remain descriptive. Moreover, the absence of later-decade examples limits any account of how this discourse is realised there.

### Overall interpretation

**Everyday interpersonal negotiation and emotional vulnerability** is the most defensible label because it integrates the full loading profile with the examples without reducing the pole to their dominant romantic theme. In this curated verbal-transcript subcorpus, the pole captures emotionally charged efforts to manage connection, recognition, boundaries, and support, especially in the 2000s stratum. It does not establish corresponding patterns in the videos’ sonic or visual meaning.
