## Factor f5 (positive): interpersonal stance and expressive self-presentation

**The most defensible interpretation is a heterogeneous pole of interpersonal stance and expressive self-presentation, with a secondary emphasis on brief moments and immediate interaction.** This should remain a provisional label: the loading vocabulary and the excerpts do not converge on a single, sharply bounded discourse.

### Possible labels

1. **Interpersonal stance and expressive self-presentation** — the preferred broad label. It accommodates attraction, sexual agency, emotional vulnerability, confidence, and the presentation or evaluation of personal qualities.
2. **Relational feeling and immediate interaction** — foregrounds the relationship discourse illustrated by the excerpts alongside the prominent *few–second* vocabulary.
3. **Personal spirit and gendered social encounter** — more closely reflects *spirit, gentleman, chick,* and *lady*, but is less securely supported by the examples. “Spirit” should mean personal character or expressive essence here, not necessarily religious spirituality.

### Lexical evidence

The full-subcorpus loadings suggest several overlapping strands:

| Lexical strand | Loading words | Interpretive contribution |
|---|---|---|
| Personal essence and affective evaluation | *spirit* (.90), *sad* (.49), *sick* (.32) | Personal qualities, emotional states, and evaluative stance, although their meanings remain context-dependent |
| Gendered social reference | *gentleman* (.61), *chick* (.47), *lady* (.36) | Social address, gendered person-reference, and potentially courtship or sexual interaction |
| Limited quantity and momentary framing | *few* (.69), *second* (.62) | Small quantities, brief intervals, and—in some contexts—immediate action |
| Imagery and movement | *cloud* (.38), *drive* (.34) | Possible atmospheric or mobility-related elements, insufficiently contextualised to define the pole |

The exceptionally strong loading of **spirit** must receive substantial interpretive weight. Nevertheless, this word alone does not establish a spiritual or religious discourse. Likewise, *few* and *second* are prominent but semantically general: their co-loading may partly reflect recurrent phrases rather than a unified topic.

### What the excerpts contribute

The examples repeatedly illustrate **relational negotiation and self-positioning**:

- **“Goodies”** presents competing sexual claims and the assertion of boundaries. Its listed loading words occur together in “a **few seconds**,” framing a boast about rapid sexual success. The transcript also visibly contains *sick, lady, drive,* and *chick*, connecting evaluation, gendered reference, and status display.
- **“Work It”** develops sexual confidence and bodily self-presentation. The listed *few* occurs in “lost a **few** pounds”; visible *chick* and *drive* contribute to competitive self-presentation and material confidence.
- **“Say My Name”** stages suspicion, demands for honesty, and relational accountability. “Two **seconds** ago” supplies evidence for challenging a partner’s account.
- **“Time After Time”** combines separation and vulnerability with reassurance. Here, **second** belongs to “the second hand,” linking the word to clock time and remembered intimacy.
- **“What’s Love Got to Do with It”** frames attraction through emotional caution and self-protection. Its repeated “**second hand emotion**” uses *second* differently: it is not a brief-time expression.
- **“American Boy”** combines attraction, travel, fashion, and confident persona-building. “I heard your lyrics, I feel your **spirit**” interprets spirit as expressive character conveyed through words—the clearest supplied context for the strongest loading.

Other excerpts qualify this interpretation. In **“Hey Ya!”**, “a **few seconds**” introduces audience participation; in **“Maps”**, “just a **second**” belongs to production instructions rather than the sung relationship discourse. **“Paper Planes”** uses *second* to frame rapid action, while **“Personal Jesus”** uses “**second best**” within a discourse of care, confession, and faith.

Thus, the examples support interpersonal and expressive discourse, but also reveal **polysemy and phrase-level heterogeneity**. Their wider themes should not automatically be treated as meanings encoded by the loading words.

### Decade pattern

The authoritative decade means rank as follows:

- **2000s:** 0.0223 — strongest positive mean
- **2020s:** 0.0053 — slightly positive
- **2010s:** −0.0340 — least aligned with this positive pole

The **2000s provide the clearest positive association**, and all supplied examples come from that stratum. However, the mean differences are small relative to the within-decade standard deviations (approximately 0.36–0.47). They suggest a modest decline in the 2010s followed by a partial recovery in the 2020s, not a strong or monotonic historical shift. Statistical significance cannot be inferred from this table alone.

### Overall judgement

**“Interpersonal stance and expressive self-presentation”** is the best working label. It balances the corpus-wide emphasis on spirit, affective evaluation, and gendered reference with the examples’ attraction, boundary-setting, confidence, and relational vulnerability. A subtitle—**“with momentary and idiomatic framing”**—would acknowledge the substantial contribution of *few* and *second* without imposing a falsely unified temporal discourse.

This interpretation concerns lexical patterns in the selected verbal transcripts, not the complete musical, sonic, or visual meanings of the videos.
