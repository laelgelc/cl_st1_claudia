## Factor f2 (positive): embodied flirtation and nightlife self-assertion

**Recommended label: “Embodied flirtation and nightlife self-assertion.”**

This pole encodes discourse in which speakers present themselves and others as attractive, sexually desirable, socially confident, and available—or selectively unavailable—for encounters. Bodily movement, gendered address, flirtation, and the negotiation of sexual attention are central. Clubs and parties frequently provide the setting, although the pole also accommodates less sexual discourse about participation and belonging in these spaces.

The label combines the strongest lexical evidence with the range of discourse illustrated by the excerpts. It does not imply that every high-scoring text expresses all these features.

### 1. Lexical basis

The strongest loadings form a relatively compact cluster:

- **chick (.85), lady (.50), gentleman (.62):** gendered categorisation and address, compatible with encounters, attraction, and the presentation of social roles. *Chick* particularly supports an informal register.
- **hip (.76), sexy (.75):** bodily display and explicit evaluations of attractiveness. In the examples, *hip* occurs as anatomical *hip/hips*, connecting the pole to dancing, movement, and erotic attention—not simply to being fashionable.
- **sick (.80):** a strong but semantically variable contributor. “A sick reputation” supports boastful self-presentation, whereas “home sick” in excerpt 002 expresses discomfort and displacement. Its loading should therefore not be treated as unambiguous evidence of sexual confidence or slang approval.

The remaining words provide less specific support. **Lovin (.31)** is compatible with intimacy; **few (.48)** and **already (.43)** occur within encounter narratives but are not themselves diagnostic of a particular discourse. **Spirit (.30)** offers insufficient evidence here for a distinct spiritual interpretation. **Gentleman**, despite its substantial loading, is not illustrated in the supplied passages, so its contribution is best understood provisionally as part of the gendered interpersonal vocabulary.

Taken together, the loadings support **informal, gendered, bodily interpersonal discourse**. The excerpts establish how this vocabulary functions in flirtation, nightlife, sexual negotiation, and self-presentation.

### 2. Discourses illustrated by the high-scoring texts

**Erotic bodily display and attraction.**  
Excerpt 003 links **hip, sexy, lady** to dancing and mutual attraction: “my hips don’t lie,” “reading the signs of my body,” and “she’s so sexy.” The body is represented as communicating desire. Excerpt 007 similarly combines **hip** and **sexy** in “look at those hips” and “get your sexy on,” constructing attractiveness as something actively displayed and performed. Excerpt 009 extends this pattern through female expressions of desire directed towards men, supported by **hip** and **sexy**.

**Sexual initiative, selectivity, and relationship demands.**  
The pole is not simply a discourse of sexual availability. In excerpt 001, **chick, sick, sexy, lady** occur within competing claims about desirability and access. Masculine boasting—“Ain’t no chick in here that I can’t have”—is met by refusal and selective partner criteria: “Sexy, independent” and “these goodies … are not / Just for any” man. Excerpt 005 uses **chick, hip, lady** in explicit sexual self-presentation and encouragement of women’s agency. Excerpt 006 combines **lady** and **hip** with post-breakup independence and demands for commitment. These examples justify including **self-assertion and negotiation**, rather than labelling the pole only “seduction.”

**Nightlife sociability and status display.**  
Excerpts 004 and 010 place **chick/lady**, and in 004 **sick**, within club encounters, conversational advances, sexual boasting, and references to consumption and material status. These contexts connect attraction with public demonstrations of confidence and success. Such status meanings are strongly illustrated by the excerpts, although wealth-related vocabulary is not itself among the supplied loadings.

**Party participation and belonging.**  
Excerpt 002 is an important qualification. Its **chick, sick, hip** vocabulary accompanies anxiety about fitting into an unfamiliar social environment, followed by reassurance through familiar songs and dancing. This broadens the pole beyond erotic encounters to **embodied participation in party culture**.

One evidential limitation concerns excerpt 008: its annotation lists **sexy** and **lady**, but neither appears in the displayed passage. That passage illustrates stylistic boasting, but it cannot directly substantiate those particular loading-word associations.

The **2000 stratum drives this positive pole most strongly**: it has the highest mean, and all ten supplied high-scoring examples belong to it. Its mean is nevertheless only slightly above zero, indicating relative prominence rather than pervasive dominance across that stratum.

The scores suggest a **decline from 2000 to 2010**, followed by a **small recovery in 2020** that remains below the 2000 level. Given the substantial within-decade standard deviations and the absence of inferential tests, this is a descriptive tendency, not evidence of a statistically established historical shift.

### 4. Possible labels

1. **Embodied flirtation and nightlife self-assertion** — best overall fit: captures bodily attraction, club sociability, boasting, initiative, and selective refusal.
2. **Gendered attraction and sexual negotiation** — foregrounds the gendered lexical cluster and the negotiation of desire, access, and commitment.
3. **Party sociability and performed desirability** — accommodates dancing and social belonging alongside erotic self-presentation.

The first label is preferable because it integrates the lexical cluster and the examples without reducing the pole to either sexual conquest or generic partying. This interpretation concerns **verbal discourse in the curated transcript subcorpus**, not the complete sonic or visual meanings of the videos or music culture as a whole.
