## Factor f2 (positive): possible labels

**Recommended label: “Desire, vulnerability and socially situated self-presentation”**

Two alternatives are:

- **“Imagistic expressions of longing and social identity”** — emphasises the use of colour, clothing and temporal imagery to articulate emotional experience and social positioning.
- **“Personal desire, change and public display”** — foregrounds the movement between pleading, loss or endurance, and narratives of appearance, recognition and belonging.

These are deliberately broad labels. The pole appears to bring together **emotionally vulnerable expressions of desire and change** with **socially situated presentations of identity and appearance**. Neither a purely romantic label nor a fashion/status label adequately covers both the loadings and the examples.

## 1. Lexical basis of the interpretation

The strongest loadings are **fade (1.04)** and **beg (.91)**. These give substantial weight to withdrawal, deterioration, longing and supplication. They suggest speakers confronting something they cannot fully control: disappearance, emotional distress, unmet desires or the passage of time.

A second cluster comprises **green (.70)** and **dress (.61)**. These introduce colour, appearance and material or social distinctions. However, their meanings vary considerably:

- **green** can evoke an imagined alternative, colour symbolism, money, food or clothing;
- **dress** can identify a desired person, mark social participation, or foreground the speaker’s self-presentation.

**Clock (.62)** and **part (.54)** potentially connect these clusters through time, observation, roles and belonging. Yet they too are polysemous: *clock* need not indicate time, and *part* need not indicate a social role.

The lower-loading items—**got (.37), pay (.36), welcome (.36), such (.34), freedom (.33)**—provide weaker support for possession, exchange, inclusion, evaluation and agency. They should not determine the label. In particular, **freedom** is not illustrated in the supplied excerpts, so a strong liberation interpretation would exceed the evidence.

The lexical configuration therefore supports a **broad, internally varied discourse of desire and personal positioning**, rather than one tightly bounded topic.

## 2. How the high-scoring excerpts realise the pole

### Vulnerability, longing and change

Several examples directly substantiate the strongest lexical signals:

- **t000639:** “Maybe then I’ll **fade** away” links disappearance to grief and an emotionally transformed world. **Green** appears in the sea imagery, while **dress** occurs in the description of passing girls. Colour and appearance thus become verbal resources for articulating loss.
- **t000555:** “I **beg**, I beg and plead,” “ticking **clocks**,” and “Am I a **part** of the cure?” combine supplication, temporal pressure and uncertainty about personal involvement.
- **t000433:** “my memory **fades**” and “**part** of a plan” place enduring love within anticipated ageing and bodily change.
- **t000434:** the former partner’s **dress**, coupled with “**such** a shame,” becomes a focus of imagined intimacy, separation and continuing attachment.

These examples justify an emotional component broader than sadness alone: the pole includes pleading, jealousy, endurance and attempts to make sense of change.

### Self-presentation, participation and belonging

Other excerpts foreground desire within social settings:

- **t000693:** “I **beg** of you” concerns parental permission to participate in music and dancing; **dresses** help construct an aspirational grown-up identity.
- **t000550:** being “**dressed** in all pink” with **green** shoes supports conspicuous self-styling and a challenge to expensive branded consumption.
- **t000569:** the Givenchy **dress** and collard **greens** connect luxury self-presentation with explicitly asserted cultural and familial identity.
- **t000273:** **greens** refer to money, **clock** concerns being watched or noticed, and **dressed** specifies local style. This is public recognition and regional affiliation, not principally temporal anxiety.
- **t000561:** “play your **part**” and “the grass is **greener**” connect relationship choices to competing expectations and imagined alternatives.
- **t000416:** **green** functions within colour-coded social difference, supporting a discourse of acceptance and coexistence.

These examples establish social display and belonging as substantial components, not incidental additions to an otherwise romantic pole.

The **2010 stratum has the highest mean**, followed by **2020**, with **2000** lowest. All supplied high-scoring excerpts also belong to the 2010 stratum, making it the clearest illustrated concentration of this pole.

The diachronic pattern is **an increase from 2000 to 2010, followed by a modest decline in 2020**, whose mean remains above 2000. This is not a continuous upward trend. The differences are also modest relative to the reported within-decade standard deviations; statistical significance cannot be inferred from this table alone.

These are patterns in the dataset’s assigned decade strata, not evidence about the original release periods of the songs or about music history generally.

## Overall interpretation

**“Desire, vulnerability and socially situated self-presentation”** best accommodates the pole’s strongest emotional loadings and its recurrent colour, clothing, role and recognition vocabulary. It captures how speakers express what they want, what they fear losing, and how they locate themselves among lovers, audiences and social groups.

The interpretation should remain provisional: substantial lexical polysemy and heterogeneous examples make this a relatively broad discourse dimension. Its evidence concerns the **verbal transcript subcorpus**, not the full sonic or visual meanings of the selected music videos.
