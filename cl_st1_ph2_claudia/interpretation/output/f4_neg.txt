## Factor f4 (negative pole): embodied pleasure and negotiated interpersonal engagement

**Recommended label:** **Embodied pleasure and negotiated interpersonal engagement**

This pole brings together discourse about **loving, bodily movement, musical participation, and choosing or soliciting involvement with others**. Its coherence is not simply “celebration”: the examples also include uncertain attachment, constrained choices, loneliness, and unsettling forms of welcome. A suitably broad interpretation is therefore **engagement in pleasurable or intimate activity, together with negotiation of who participates and on what terms**.

This interpretation is provisional. The eight-word loading set suggests a wider discourse than the supplied examples alone demonstrate, while those examples reveal considerable variation in how the loading words function.

### 1. Lexical evidence: loving, movement, and interpersonal negotiation

The strongest loading, **lovin (−.85)**, makes colloquial loving or intimate affection central to the lexical profile. **Hip (−.59)** and **rhythm (−.50)** add an embodied and explicitly music-related component. Depending on context, *hip* can refer to bodily movement or occur in *hip hop*; both uses are visible in the examples. Together, these words support a discourse of **intimacy and rhythmic participation**, rather than a purely abstract discussion of relationships.

A second cluster concerns the terms of engagement:

- **Beg (−.55)** suggests entreaty, dependence, or attempts to secure another’s response.
- **Choose (−.40)** foregrounds selection, agency, and decisions about participation or attachment.
- **Welcome (−.32)** introduces reception or entry into a relationship, place, or situation.

**Sunshine (−.45)** is compatible with warmth and positive affect, although its contextual meaning cannot be established from these excerpts. Similarly, **pay (−.32)** potentially adds cost, obligation, or exchange, but should not automatically be interpreted as commercial sexuality or literal payment.

Crucially, **lovin, beg, sunshine, and pay are not identified as loading words in the supplied examples**. Their contribution to the interpretation comes from the full-subcorpus loadings, not from direct illustration here.

### 2. Excerpt evidence: participation, choice, and ambivalent attachment

The examples provide especially clear support for two related discourse strands.

**Embodied musical participation.** In excerpt 006, *rhythm* occurs alongside dance instructions, bodily movement, and invitations to shared activity: “swing your hips,” “do the Loco-Motion with me,” and “holding hands.” Pleasure is also framed as emotionally restorative: dancing “makes you happy when you’re feeling blue.”

Excerpt 007 similarly places *rhythm* within repeated collective exhortations: “make your body sway,” “socialize,” and “get up and dance.” The transcript also visibly contains *hip hop*, although the supplied loading-word annotation lists only *rhythm*. This is strong textual evidence for participatory enjoyment and bodily engagement—not evidence about the video’s actual choreography or sound.

Excerpt 008 links *choose* to musical preference and a conditional invitation: “If you wanna dance with me.” Here, selection and social participation converge.

**Negotiating intimate involvement.** Several excerpts use *choose* to organise relationships:

- Excerpt 004 presents erotic autonomy: “give your love … to whoever you choose,” reinforced by “do what you wanna do.”
- Excerpt 009 combines choosing a partner with commitment, sexual relationships, and transactional or exploitative interpersonal arrangements.
- Excerpt 010 makes romantic choice consequential and potentially threatening: “choose carefully” and “if you choose to walk away.”
- Excerpt 002 gives choice an unequal emotional outcome: the addressee does what they choose while the speaker remains “alone again.”

These examples support **negotiated engagement**, rather than uncomplicated freedom or mutual affection. Excerpt 005, where the annotated *choose* is represented by “chosen,” adds attraction and absorption through dancing, but offers weaker evidence for deliberate choice.

The two *welcome* excerpts qualify the interpretation further. “Welcome home” accompanies isolation and psychological confinement in excerpt 001; “welcome to a new kind of tension” introduces political alienation in excerpt 003. Thus, *welcome* does not consistently encode hospitable inclusion. These texts are peripheral to the pleasure-and-intimacy core and show that the pole includes **ambivalent or ironic entry into situations**, not uniformly positive belonging.

### 3. Decade pattern

For this negative pole, the relevant ordering is:

| **2010** | **0.163** | Strongest |
| **2020** | **0.217** | Intermediate |

All means are positive, but **2010 is still the strongest negative-pole stratum because it has the lowest mean**. All supplied examples also belong to 2010, reinforcing its prominence in the illustrative material.

The pattern suggests greater relative prominence from 2000 to 2010, followed by a partial retreat in 2020—not a continuous diachronic increase. The differences are modest relative to the reported standard deviations, and no significance tests are supplied. These are tendencies within the curated transcript sample and its assigned decade strata, not claims about music generally or the original release dates of recognisable songs.

### Possible labels

1. **Embodied pleasure and negotiated interpersonal engagement** — best overall coverage of the lexical profile and the varied examples.
2. **Loving, dancing, and choosing involvement** — a transparent, lexically grounded label, though less analytically compact.
3. **Invitations and choices in intimate and rhythmic participation** — captures the interactional pattern, but gives less prominence to entreaty and potentially costly engagement.

The first is preferable because it accommodates both the pole’s **pleasure-oriented lexical core** and the examples’ **unequal, uncertain, or constrained forms of involvement**.
