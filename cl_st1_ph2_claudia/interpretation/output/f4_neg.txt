## Factor f4 (negative pole): Affectionate desire and relational affirmation

**Recommended label: “Affectionate desire and relational affirmation.”**

This pole brings together colloquial expressions of loving and sexual desire, appeals for emotional reciprocation, and claims about genuine feelings or desirable relationships. A secondary strand concerns personal fulfilment and liberation. The pole is therefore broader than uncomplicated romantic happiness: its examples include fidelity, persuasion, infidelity, disappointment, and self-preservation.

### Possible labels

| Label | Justification |
|---|---|
| **Affectionate desire and relational affirmation** | Best captures the combination of **lovin**, appeals for intimacy, and **true** as a marker of sincerity, fidelity, or conviction. It also accommodates relationships that are desired or defended rather than already secure. |
| **Colloquial intimacy and romantic persuasion** | Highlights the informal register suggested by **lovin**, **hip**, and **chick**, together with the pleading suggested by **beg**. Particularly appropriate for the examples that request love, physical closeness, or reassurance, but less inclusive of liberation and collective aspiration. |
| **Love, authenticity, and aspirational fulfilment** | Gives greater weight to the varied uses of **true** and to **wonderful** and **happy**. It accommodates both romantic fulfilment and the examples concerned with self-authenticity, a better life, or collective harmony, though it captures colloquial sensuality less directly. |

## Lexical evidence

The strongest loading, **lovin (-1.07)**, provides the clearest anchor: colloquially expressed affection and intimacy, often with a sensual meaning. **Hip (-.66)** and **chick (-.61)** suggest an informal vocabulary of social identity and gendered attraction. However, neither appears in the supplied excerpts, so their precise discourse functions remain less securely illustrated.

The weaker-loading items broaden this pattern:

- **Beg (-.37)** suggests pleading or solicitation, consistent with appeals for love and reciprocation.
- **Wonderful (-.37)** and **happy (-.33)** contribute positive evaluation and anticipated pleasure or fulfilment.
- **True (-.32)** introduces sincerity, fidelity, authenticity, and assertions of conviction.
- **Welcome (-.37)** adds an affiliative or audience-addressing element, illustrated by a spoken performance introduction.

Taken together, these loadings support a discourse of **informal affective engagement**, especially around intimacy and desirable emotional states. They do not support treating every text at this pole as either sexually explicit or uniformly celebratory.

## Evidence from the excerpts

### Affection and sexual desire as needs to be expressed or satisfied

The clearest illustration is t000938: “nothing wrong with me **lovin’** you” and “if the love is **true**” combine sensual invitation with an affirmation of love’s legitimacy. The surrounding requests—“let’s get it on” and “let your love come out”—make this a discourse of persuasive intimacy. **Wonderful** and **happy** also occur visibly in the transcript, although they are not included in its supplied loading-word inventory.

In t000968, repeated **lovin’** presents intimacy as a remedy: “All I … really need / Gimme that good lovin’.” Requests, a “plea,” and the fever/cure metaphor reinforce the connection between desire, emotional need, and anticipated wellbeing.

### Sincerity, fidelity, and imagined romantic fulfilment

Several excerpts contextualise **true** within romantic commitment:

- t000945: “a love so rare and **true**”;
- t000946: “a heart that’s **true**,” alongside requests for reconciliation and marriage;
- t000950: plans that “come **true**,” linked to exclusive love and lifelong companionship.

These examples support relational affirmation, but also show that **true** is polysemous: it can describe faithful love or the fulfilment of a wish. In t000883, “Can’t believe … it’s **true**” occurs within a strongly sensual declaration of attraction.

### Authenticity and fulfilment beyond romantic reassurance

The remaining examples prevent an overly narrow interpretation. In t000892, “to yourself be **true**” supports self-preservation against an unsatisfying relationship. In t000896, “the mind’s **true** liberation” accompanies collective harmony and visionary hope. In t000953, “one thing I know is **true**” asserts conviction within a narrative of hardship and hoped-for escape.

The infidelity narrative in t000857 further complicates any simple sincerity label: **true** modifies “player,” while **welcome** belongs to audience address. This supports a secondary performance-affiliation strand rather than sincere romance alone.

Most supplied examples illustrate only **true**, so their thematic diversity should not displace the full loading profile, especially the prominence of **lovin**.

Nevertheless, all means are positive, and their differences are small relative to the reported standard deviations. The pattern suggests a **weak, non-monotonic tendency**, not a demonstrated historical increase. The examples’ decade assignments should be retained as corpus metadata rather than reclassified according to apparent song origins.

Overall, **“Affectionate desire and relational affirmation”** offers the best balance between the loadings and examples. It identifies a lexical discourse pattern in this curated verbal-transcript subcorpus, not a comprehensive account of music-video meaning or musical history.
