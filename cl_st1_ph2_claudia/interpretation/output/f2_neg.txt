## Factor f2 (negative pole): social address, desirability and evaluative stance

**Recommended label: “Social address, desirability and evaluative stance.”**

This pole combines discourse that **presents or evaluates socially visible identities**, **addresses partners or audiences**, and **expresses attraction, confidence, discomfort or disapproval**. Sexual desirability is a prominent component, but it does not adequately describe the whole pole: the strong loading of *gentleman* and the varied uses of *sick* identify additional audience-address and evaluative strands.

### 1. What the loadings indicate

The strongest loadings suggest several overlapping lexical components:

| Component | Loading evidence | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Desirability and gendered identity | *sexy* (−.75), *gentleman* (−.71), *lady* (−.40) | Naming, addressing or evaluating people through socially recognisable identities |
| Evaluative and affective stance | *stupid* (−.60), *sick* (−.55) | Judgements of people, situations or personal states; potentially positive slang as well as negative evaluation |
| Interpersonal negotiation | *question* (−.51) | Uncertainty, questioning and the negotiation of responses |
| Embodied or rhythmic participation | *hip* (−.50), *rhythm* (−.43) | A possible bodily and music-related component, supported more selectively by the excerpts |
| Supplementary associations | *few* (−.37), *quick* (−.34), *gang* (−.30) | Timing, quantity and group reference, but insufficiently specific to determine the label |

The near-equal prominence of *sexy* and *gentleman* matters. A label restricted to sexual attraction would privilege one major loading while overlooking another. Equally, *sick* should not be assigned a single meaning across the subcorpus.

### 2. What the examples establish

**Desirability and interpersonal negotiation are strongly illustrated.** In excerpt 001, “if you think I’m **sexy**” solicits an explicit response to attraction, while “avoiding all the **questions**” situates that attraction within uncertainty and courtship. Excerpt 003 combines **sexy** with **sick** in “a sick reputation”: desirability intersects with boastful self-presentation, partner selection and refusal of sexual access. Excerpt 004 repeatedly uses **sexy** as a claim and injunction—“bringing sexy back” and “get your sexy on”—making attractiveness something asserted and enacted verbally.

These examples support a discourse of **performed desirability**, but not simply sexual availability. They include requests, competitive claims, preferences and boundaries.

**Public audience address forms a distinct second strand.** In excerpts 002, 006 and 009, the loading item **gentleman** is represented by “ladies and gentlemen,” used to introduce performers. Here it functions as a conventional address formula, not primarily as a description of courteous masculinity. The surrounding announcements present performers to an audience and manage attention. This establishes a **public-presentational register** within the verbal transcripts.

**Evaluation and discomfort broaden the pole further.** The examples show several meanings of **sick**:

- positive reputational slang in excerpt 003;
- physical or emotional discomfort in excerpt 002;
- homesickness and anxiety in excerpt 005;
- emphatic rejection in “I’m so sick of it!” in excerpt 010.

The last example concerns political violence rather than courtship or social display. Consequently, evaluative stance is an important component, not merely an accessory to the sexual-desirability examples.

There are also evidential limits. *Stupid, rhythm, quick* and *gang* are not illustrated in the supplied passages, so their corpus-wide associations should not be reconstructed from these samples alone. Excerpt 008 is annotated for *sick*, but that word does not occur in the displayed portion; it therefore provides no direct contextual evidence for its use here.

### 3. Decade pattern

For this negative pole, the authoritative ordering is:

1. **2000:** 0.0669 — strongest relative association  
2. **2020:** 0.1640  
3. **2010:** 0.1985 — weakest relative association  

All means are positive, but the **lowest mean identifies the decade closest to the negative pole**. The 2000 stratum therefore drives this pole most strongly, consistent with all ten supplied examples belonging to that stratum.

The pattern suggests reduced average prominence from 2000 to 2010, followed by a modest return toward the pole in 2020. It is **not a monotonic decline**. The mean differences are small relative to the reported standard deviations, so these figures support a descriptive tendency, not a demonstrated significant historical shift. The supplied corpus decade assignments should be retained regardless of the apparent historical provenance of individual songs.

### Possible labels

- **Social address, desirability and evaluative stance** — best overall coverage of the major loadings and heterogeneous examples.
- **Performed desirability and public audience address** — foregrounds the two strongest lexical anchors, *sexy* and *gentleman*, but captures the diverse *sick* examples less fully.
- **Interpersonal positioning and expressive evaluation** — accommodates attraction, self-presentation, questioning, discomfort and disapproval, although it is less lexically specific.

Overall, this is best treated as a **composite lexical-discourse pole**, rather than a uniformly sexual or party-oriented dimension. The interpretation concerns verbal patterns in the curated transcript subcorpus, not the complete musical, sonic or visual meanings of the videos.
