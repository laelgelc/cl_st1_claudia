## Factor f2 (negative): personal change, relational longing and social self-presentation

**Recommended label:** **Personal change and relational experience**

This pole brings together discourses of **longing, attachment, vulnerability and changing personal circumstances**, often expressed through concrete references to appearance, surroundings and time. It also includes **social self-presentation and participation**—dressing, dancing, choosing partners and occupying social roles. The evidence therefore supports a broad experiential dimension rather than a narrowly romantic, melancholic or celebratory one.

### Possible labels

| Label | Justification |
|---|---|
| **Personal change and relational experience** | Best overall coverage: accommodates fading, ageing, growth, emotional dependence, romantic choice and changing social circumstances. |
| **Relational longing and embodied self-presentation** | Emphasises the combination of *beg* and *somebody* with *dress* and the excerpts’ attention to attraction, clothing and public identity. Less successful for the environmental imagery associated with *green* and *fade*. |
| **Attachment, transience and social roles** | Highlights enduring or threatened attachment, temporal change and the varied meanings of *part*. Useful as a more explicitly composite label, although not every occurrence of *part* denotes a social role. |

### Lexical evidence

The strongest loadings—**fade (−.83), part (−.76) and green (−.73)**—should anchor the interpretation, rather than allowing the love-oriented examples alone to determine the label.

- **Fade**, together with **grow (−.33)** and **clock (−.52)**, supports a discourse of change, duration and transience. In the examples, this includes fading memory, imagined disappearance, ageing and the pressure of “ticking clocks.” These meanings encompass both deterioration and persistence through change.
- **Beg (−.61)** and **somebody (−.45)** contribute interpersonal need, appeal and desire. Their contexts range from requesting parental permission to emotional pleading, sexual anticipation and seeking a person to love.
- **Dress (−.49)** grounds personal experience in visible appearance and social presentation: teenage maturity, wedding-related banter, regional clothing styles, mourning and tears.
- **Part** is semantically broad. “Part of the cure,” “part of a plan,” “play your part” and “part of the game” concern involvement, belonging, explanation or assigned conduct. It should not automatically be interpreted as separation.
- **Green** is similarly heterogeneous: a “green sea” provides environmental imagery, “grass is greener” expresses comparative opportunity, and “greens” refers to money. Its strong loading supports the pole’s lexical importance, but not a single ecological meaning.

The lower-loading **sunshine, stare, freedom and lord** suggest additional possibilities involving setting, looking, agency and invocation. They do not, on the supplied evidence, justify treating this as primarily a religious, liberation or nature discourse.

### Evidence from the excerpts

The examples demonstrate several connected, but not identical, discourse strands.

**Vulnerability, loss and the need for connection.**  
In excerpt 002, “I **beg** … and plead,” “ticking **clocks**” and the question of being **part** of a cure or disease combine distress, temporal pressure and uncertain personal agency. Excerpt 004 links “**fade** away” with bereavement and a transformed “**green** sea”; **dress** occurs within the surrounding scene of people and summer clothing. Excerpt 009 makes interpersonal need explicit through repeated requests for “**somebody** to love,” while tears running down a **dress** embody that distress.

**Attachment across time and changing circumstances.**  
Excerpt 005 provides a particularly clear conjunction of **fade, part and grow**: memory fades, the soul cannot grow old, and love may be part of a plan. The discourse concerns continuity of attachment despite bodily and cognitive change. Excerpt 010 places **dress** and **grow** within a retrospective account of class judgement, separation, travel and persistent romantic memory.

**Desire, appearance and social participation.**  
Excerpt 001 combines **beg, dress and somebody** in a narrative of adolescent aspiration, parental permission and public entertainment. Excerpt 003 connects **part, green and dress** with marriage, partner selection, peer pressure and competing models of masculinity. Excerpt 007 combines romantic affirmation and sexual desire with spoken coaching, showing that **part** need not belong to the same discourse strand as **beg** or **somebody**. Excerpt 006 foregrounds partying, regional affiliation, money and clothing: **green, clock and dress** here support status and public identity rather than introspective loss.

These differences matter: **melancholy and enduring love are prominent, but they do not exhaust the pole**.

### Decade pattern and diachronic interpretation

The **2010s drive the negative pole most strongly**:

- **2010:** −0.2212  
- **2020:** −0.1958  
- **2000:** +0.0260  

The means suggest a movement towards this lexical-discourse configuration between the 2000s and 2010s, followed by broadly similar positioning in the 2020s, with a slight weakening. The latter difference is small relative to the reported standard deviations; statistical significance cannot be established from this table alone.

All supplied examples belong to the **2010 stratum**, reinforcing its illustrative prominence but leaving the 2020 pattern without direct excerpt evidence. These are corpus-stratum assignments, not necessarily dates of original song composition or release.

Overall, **Personal change and relational experience** is the most defensible label: it captures the strongest temporal and experiential loadings while accommodating the excerpts’ interpersonal longing, embodied presentation and negotiation of social identity. This interpretation concerns lexical patterns in the curated verbal transcripts, not the complete musical or visual meanings of the videos.
