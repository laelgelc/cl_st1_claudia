## Factor f3 (negative): Embodied desire and intimate address

### Possible labels

1. **Embodied desire and intimate address** — the strongest overall label. It captures both the pole’s bodily vocabulary and the interpersonal discourse of attraction, sensual contact, and longing.
2. **Sensual attraction and romantic attachment** — foregrounds the range from sexual invitation to emotionally invested relationships and separation.
3. **Bodily intimacy and sexual self-assertion** — highlights the more explicit, confident performances of desire, although it fits the romantic-loss examples less fully.

The pole is best interpreted as **discourse that makes attraction and attachment tangible through bodies, contact, and expressive vocalisation**. It includes flirtation, sexual invitation, desirability, and longing for a partner’s physical presence. It is not exclusively a discourse of explicit sexuality.

### Lexical evidence

The strongest loadings establish an embodied and affectively expressive core:

| Loading | Interpretive contribution |
|---|---|
| **mmm (−.85)** | A transcribed vocalisation that can convey pleasure, anticipation, or affective engagement. Its prominence also indicates a vocal-performance component, although it is not inherently sexual. |
| **lip (−.75)** | Directs attention to bodily intimacy, kissing, and the sexually attractive body. |
| **handle (−.72)** | Can express sexual capability or managing an encounter, but its meaning varies substantially across contexts. |
| **touch (−.57)** | Links physical contact with desire, intimacy, and emotional attachment. |

The remaining words provide less uniform support. **Chick (−.55)** can contribute gendered, informal reference to desirable women; **stare (−.42)** can encode sustained attention to a desired person. **Question (−.31)** appears in an example of uncertainty during courtship, but it is not intrinsically an intimacy marker. **Few (−.32)** and **cloud (−.40)** have no clear, stable thematic contribution on the supplied evidence.

Thus, the interpretation rests on the **co-occurrence pattern**, not on treating all nine words as synonyms for desire. In particular, the strong loading of *handle* must be retained without assuming that every occurrence is sexual.

### Evidence from the excerpts

The examples reveal several related forms of embodied interpersonal discourse.

- **Intimacy remembered and desired:** In excerpt 001, “have your **touch**, or kiss your **lips**” makes separation concrete through the absence of bodily contact. The opening **mmm** accompanies a discourse of regret, dependence, and reunion. This example supports *romantic attachment* as well as sensuality.

- **Attraction negotiated through bodily cues:** Excerpt 002 combines **mmm** with “licking your **lips**,” while the surrounding lyrics distinguish bodily desire from emotional hesitation. Intimacy is conditional rather than automatic. Excerpt 003 similarly combines dry **lips**, nervous courtship, and the invitation to “reach out and **touch** me.” Its “questions” also illustrates the weaker *question* loading in a courtship context.

- **Sexual appraisal and confident invitation:** Excerpt 006 combines **mmm** and **lips** with explicit appraisal of a desired man and assertive pursuit. Excerpt 008 places **mmm** and “I had to **handle** that” within a sexualised club encounter. In excerpt 010, **lips** and “you think you can **handle** this” accompany bodily display and sexual self-confidence; **chick** and **few** also occur, though their local contributions differ considerably.

- **Contact as emotional intensity:** Excerpt 009 repeatedly links **touch** to overwhelming romantic feeling, with **stare** appearing in sustained attention to the partner’s eyes. However, its **handle** occurrence belongs to a boastful basketball comparison, not bodily intimacy. This demonstrates the semantic heterogeneity behind the factor.

Not all examples fit the central discourse equally well. Excerpt 004 uses **lips** in grotesque, provocative bodily humour, while excerpt 005 is largely self-assertive rather than intimate. These suggest that bodily reference and performative expressiveness can also draw texts toward this pole outside conventional romance.

There are also discrepancies between some supplied loading-word lists and the visible excerpts: for example, **touch** is listed for excerpts 004 and 005 but is not visible in either passage, and neither listed word is visible in excerpt 007. Those listed matches should not be treated as contextual evidence within the displayed passages.

### Decade pattern

For this negative pole, the **2000 stratum is the strongest driver**:

| Decade stratum | Mean f3 |
|---|---:|
| 2000 | **−0.149** |
| 2010 | +0.072 |
| 2020 | +0.196 |

The means increase monotonically, indicating **progressively weaker average association with this negative-pole lexical configuration** from 2000 to 2020. All ten supplied examples belong to the 2000 stratum, reinforcing that pattern.

However, the mean differences are modest relative to the within-decade standard deviations, approximately **0.63–0.67**. Without significance tests, this is a descriptive tendency, not evidence that intimacy discourse disappeared or that every later transcript is less sensual. The supplied stratum assignments—not inferred song-release dates—govern this comparison.

### Recommended interpretation

**Embodied desire and intimate address** is the most defensible label. It accommodates sensual courtship, sexual self-assertion, and emotionally charged longing while recognising bodily humour, vocalisation, and semantically variable words as part of the broader lexical pattern. This interpretation concerns the **verbal discourse of the curated transcript sample**, not the total sonic or visual meanings of the music videos.
