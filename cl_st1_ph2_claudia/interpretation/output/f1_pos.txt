## Factor f1 (positive): colloquial sexual-social performance and self-assertion

The positive pole most plausibly encodes **informal, action-oriented discourse in which speakers perform desirability, social confidence, and interpersonal control**. Its central activities are flirting, negotiating sexual access, directing bodily movement, and asserting status or competence. The excerpts frequently situate these activities in parties, clubs, and encounters between potential partners, but the full loading profile also accommodates everyday exchanges, performance talk, and confrontational self-positioning.

### Possible labels

1. **Colloquial sexual-social performance and self-assertion** — **preferred**.  
   This captures both the strongly illustrated sexual and social encounters and the broader vocabulary of confidence, action, and competence.

2. **Flirtation, bodily display, and status negotiation**.  
   This foregrounds the dominant thematic combination in the excerpts: attraction, dance or bodily movement, and claims about who is desirable, capable, or in control. It is somewhat narrower than the full loading profile.

3. **Vernacular interaction and performative confidence**.  
   This accommodates conversational exchanges, audience address, boasting, and performance discourse alongside seduction. However, it makes the particularly prominent bodily and sexual component less explicit.

## Lexical basis

The 33 loading words suggest several interconnected discourse functions rather than a single topic.

- **Gendered address and attraction:** *chick* (.73), *lady* (.42), and *girl* (.34), together with *sexy* (.60), *hip* (.70), and *dress* (.36), support discourse organised around potential partners, appearance, and bodily desirability. In the examples, *hip* refers to the body; its loading should not itself be treated as evidence of a hip-hop genre label.
- **Movement, action, and readiness:** *drop* (.72), *ready* (.60), *act* (.59), *drive* (.58), *quick* (.54), *work* (.50), *step* (.39), and *take* (.32) give the pole an active, directive orientation. Their meanings vary across bodily movement, sexual action, practical activity, and figurative expressions.
- **Informal interaction:** *yo* (.71), *got* (.64), *okay* (.43), *gotta* (.40), *cause* (.38), *talk* (.36), and *phone* (.42) support a colloquial, socially engaged register. The excerpts realise this through greetings, conversational turns, invitations, challenges, and direct address.
- **Confidence, competence, and confrontation:** *game* (.53), *cool* (.40), and *real* (.40) contribute to performances of social skill and credibility. *Fight* (.35) and *gang* (.33) extend the profile toward confrontation and group affiliation, while *pay* (.54), *work*, and *job* (.31) leave room for practical or economic activity.

These words require contextual interpretation. For example, *pay* occurs in “pay attention,” and *drive* can express attraction through “driving me crazy.” The pole therefore cannot be reduced to money, vehicles, or employment simply because those lexical items load on it.

## Evidence from the high-scoring excerpts

The examples strongly substantiate the interactional and sexual interpretation, while also revealing differences in speaker positioning.

**Seduction as a display of competence** is especially clear in t000152. The speaker approaches a woman, claims to know what “the ladies like,” and receives the evaluation “your **game** is tight.” Loading words including **chick, lady, girl, ready, cool, real**, and **take** connect attraction with conversational confidence, readiness, and self-presentation.

**Sexual access is negotiated rather than uniformly granted.** In t000146, flirtation includes questions about respect, challenges to presumed intentions, and reciprocal claims of desirability. In t000158, **girl, chick, ready, drive, talk**, and **already** occur within a contest between sexual boasting and refusal: “Just because you **drive** a Benz / I’m not going home with you.” The pole thus encompasses both pursuit and boundary-setting, not merely conquest.

**Bodily display and movement become performances of desirability.** In t000176 and t000141, **drop, step, ready, work**, and **girl** connect dancing with sexual invitation. In t000010, **hip, work, phone, chick, game**, and **drive** combine explicit sexual agency, partner contact, bodily self-display, and competitive confidence. These examples also show that assertive sexual positioning is not exclusively male.

**Status and credibility intersect with attraction.** In t000130, **drop, game, real, drive**, and **lady** accompany claims about possessions, sexual influence, and street authority. Yet t000059 broadens the interpretation: **yo, game, okay, phone, ready**, and **work** occur in spoken exchanges, a card-game episode, and discourse about performing and celebrity attention. This supports retaining an interactional-performance component in the label rather than calling the pole simply “club sexuality.”

## Decade pattern and diachronic tendency

| Decade | N | Mean f1 |

| 2000 | 269 | 3.227 |

The **2000 stratum has the strongest positive-pole association**. All ten supplied high-scoring excerpts are assigned to that stratum, reinforcing its importance in the illustrative evidence.

The means decline monotonically from 2000 to 2010 to 2020, with the larger decrease occurring between the first two strata. This suggests that this lexical configuration becomes **less prominent on average in the later sampled transcripts**, while remaining positively represented throughout.

The substantial within-decade standard deviations caution against treating any decade as homogeneous. Without inferential results or later-decade excerpts, the pattern does not establish statistical significance or explain what drives the decrease.

**Overall, f1 positive is best labelled “Colloquial sexual-social performance and self-assertion”: a sampled transcript discourse of attraction, bodily action, interpersonal negotiation, and performed confidence, most strongly associated with the 2000 stratum.** This interpretation concerns verbal discourse in the curated sample, not the complete musical, sonic, or visual meanings of the videos.
