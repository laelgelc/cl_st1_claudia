## Factor f3 (positive): Freedom, hardship and the search for fulfilment

**The most defensible interpretation is a discourse of seeking freedom and fulfilment within everyday social, emotional and spiritual life.** This includes collective resistance, release from restrictive relationships, movement and independence, romantic happiness and loss, and devotional longing. It is a heterogeneous pole: the evidence supports these related strands more strongly than a single narrowly defined discourse.

### Possible labels

1. **Freedom, hardship and the search for fulfilment** — the preferred label, encompassing political and personal freedom alongside emotional difficulty, happiness and spiritual aspiration.
2. **Personal and collective release, belonging and longing** — foregrounds the movement between assertions of independence and desires for companionship, solidarity or spiritual connection.
3. **Everyday struggles and aspirations for freedom and happiness** — gives greater prominence to the strong loading of *job* and the ordinary-life settings of several excerpts, although the occupational strand is only sparsely illustrated.

These labels describe this pole alone.

## Lexical evidence

The strongest loading, **freedom (.80)**, provides the clearest semantic anchor. Its contexts in the examples demonstrate that freedom is not exclusively political: it also concerns relational release, personal independence and mobility.

The equally substantial loadings of **bear (.77)** and **job (.77)** mean that the interpretation cannot rest on freedom alone. *Job* introduces work and livelihood into the lexical profile. *Bear*, however, is not illustrated in the supplied excerpts, so its contribution remains uncertain: interpreting it specifically as enduring hardship would require concordance evidence rather than relying on that possible sense.

**Gang (.71)** adds a group-oriented component, but its meanings vary. In the excerpts, it occurs as an informal audience address and in the restrictive image of a “chain gang.” It therefore does not justify labelling the whole pole as gang-related or criminal discourse.

**Happy (.58)** and **sad (.43)** support an emotional strand encompassing desired happiness, remembered fulfilment and present distress. **Lord (.45)** contributes both devotional address and affective exclamation. The additional words **gentleman (.53), road (.45), rhythm (.39), and lady (.32)** suggest social address or person-reference, movement, and musical activity. Their contextual contribution is less securely established by the examples, particularly for *gentleman* and *lady*.

Overall, the loadings suggest a combination of **agency, everyday social circumstances and emotional or spiritual aspiration**, rather than a narrowly political, romantic or religious dimension.

## How the excerpts instantiate the pole

The examples give several distinct realisations of this lexical pattern:

- **Collective freedom and resistance:** t000927 explicitly connects *freedom* to speech and resistance to “the powers that be.” The visible word *rhythm* situates that assertion within verbal references to musical performance. In t000906, *gang* occurs in “chain gang,” framing constrained lives and opposition to external power; “happiest days” supplies a contrasting memory of fulfilment.

- **Personal freedom, mobility and loss:** t000934 combines *freedom* and *lord* with travel, companionship and separation. Freedom is presented ambivalently as having “nothing left to lose.” In t000973, *freedom* concerns unequal autonomy after a relationship ends: the speaker demands release from continuing emotional control. In t000988, freedom is explicitly valued within a narrative of roaming and searching.

- **Emotional and spiritual fulfilment:** t000902 combines the address *gang* with *lord* and the visible loading word *sad*, locating the pole in romantic loss and loneliness. Conversely, t000811 promises to make a partner *happy*. In t000984, repeated *lord* is genuinely devotional, expressing a desire for spiritual closeness rather than functioning merely as an exclamation.

- **Everyday livelihood and shared aspiration:** t000817 illustrates *job* in spoken domestic dialogue about work and growing up. This establishes an ordinary social-life component, but one brief occurrence cannot define the entire pole as occupational discourse. In t000974, *happy* appears alongside work, departure and an appeal to come together.

Some supplied loading-word lists do not fully match the visible excerpts: for example, *happy* is listed for t000927 but is not visible, whereas *rhythm* is. Interpretive claims should therefore follow the displayed wording, with those discrepancies checked against the underlying data.

## Decade pattern

| Decade | Mean f3 |
|---|---:|
| 2000 | −0.230 |
| 2010 | 0.038 |
| 2020 | 0.164 |

**The 2020 stratum is most strongly associated with this positive pole**, followed by 2010; 2000 has the lowest mean. All supplied high-scoring examples also belong to 2020, illustrating that stratum’s contribution.

The means suggest an increasing association with this lexical configuration across the three included decades. Nevertheless, the differences are modest relative to the within-decade standard deviations, so they do not establish a uniform or statistically significant shift.

This is a tendency within the **curated transcript sample and its decade assignments**, not evidence that these lyrical discourses originated in, or became universally characteristic of, the 2020s. Nor does it establish changes in sonic or visual meaning.

**Recommended label: _Freedom, hardship and the search for fulfilment_**, with moderate interpretive confidence because the examples clarify several important strands but leave the strongly loading word *bear* unresolved.
