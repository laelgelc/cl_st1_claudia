## Factor f1 (negative pole): colloquial self-assertion, flirtation and social performance

### Possible labels

1. **Colloquial self-assertion and flirtatious social performance** — the best overall label. It captures the pole’s informal, interactional language alongside recurring performances of desirability, confidence, competence and status.
2. **Hip-hop-inflected sociability, desire and status display** — a more culturally specific label, supported by the examples’ rap-oriented address, courtship exchanges, boasting and accounts of social activity. “Hip-hop-inflected” describes the discourse rather than establishing a genre classification.
3. **Assertive courtship and streetwise everyday interaction** — foregrounds sexual negotiation and practical social encounters, while accommodating everyday storytelling and claims to authenticity.

The first label is preferable because it covers the full lexical pattern without reducing the pole to sexual content, club settings or a particular musical genre.

### Lexical evidence

The loadings bring together three overlapping discourse resources:

- **Informal address and interaction:** *yo* (−.77), *got* (−.57), *okay* (−.44), *gotta* (−.41), *cool* (−.40), *cause* (−.38) and *talk* (−.37). These support a colloquial register organised around direct address, conversational responses and immediate interpersonal engagement.
- **Action, readiness and competence:** *drop* (−.75), *quick* (−.64), *ready* (−.59), *act* (−.54), *game* and *work* (both −.53), *step* (−.52), *already* (−.51), *drive* and *next* (both −.50). Collectively, these suggest doing, moving, initiating and managing situations. Their discourse functions are context-dependent: *game* can indicate seductive skill, social standing or actual gameplay; *work* can describe bodily performance rather than employment.
- **Desirability, evaluation and social positioning:** *sexy* (−.58), *real* (−.47), *crazy* (−.46), *chick* (−.42), *lady* (−.35) and *girl* (−.32). Alongside *pay*, *gang*, *fight*, *dress*, *phone* and *stare*, these extend the pole into attraction, authenticity, material resources, affiliation and interpersonal tension.

The pole therefore encodes more than a topic. It also captures a **way of presenting social experience**: informal, action-oriented and concerned with how speakers attract, impress, challenge or respond to others. Individual words should not be assigned fixed meanings: for example, *pay* also occurs in “pay attention,” while *drop* refers to performance, movement or vehicles across different examples.

### Evidence from the excerpts

**Flirtation and sexual negotiation** are especially prominent. In t000146, *girl, game, work, talk, got* and *crazy* occur within a reciprocal exchange about attraction, sexual access, respect and personal agency. In t000152, *yo, ready, game, real* and *cool* support conversational courtship intertwined with cars, clothing and claims to desirability. These are enacted social encounters, not simply references to romance.

**Embodied attraction and confident sexual agency** appear in t000081 and t000141. The former uses *yo, okay, real, work, next* and *take* in playful, assertive pursuit of men; the latter combines *drop, work, step, ready* and *girl* with dance, bodily display and spending. The pole thus accommodates different gendered speaking positions rather than exclusively masculine boasting.

**Status, authenticity and performance competence** broaden the interpretation. In t000031, *game, pay, work, real* and *act* connect desirability with social knowledge, money and credibility. In t000130, *drop, game, drive* and *real* accompany luxury display, sexual confidence and intimidation. By contrast, t000059 combines conversational dialogue and literal gameplay with claims about performing rhymes and managing celebrity attention.

The remaining examples prevent an overly narrow “sexual boasting” label. T000009 combines *crazy, got, talk* and *girl* with romantic vulnerability, alongside a self-promotional rap passage. T000127 foregrounds locality, sociability and status, while t000138 narrates everyday mobility, friendship, basketball and relief from danger.
